Scenario 01 Easy Rule: brute_force Expected severity: high

Brute Force Investigation

You have received a brute-force alert. Your job is to investigate it and determine what happened: repeated failed SSH logons against the production web server. Establish exactly what happened, and prove the attack did not succeed.

Incident story

The overnight operations report flags a burst of failed SSH authentications against web-01. A junior analyst noted "probably someone fat-fingering a password" and closed the ticket. Your task is to replace that assumption with evidence. There is a high-severity alert on the SOC Triage Board that matches this brief.

Security objective

Identify an automated credential-stuffing / brute force attack against the web-01 SSH service, quantify it precisely, and confirm whether the attacker obtained access.

What you know at the start

  • The affected host is a Linux web server reachable over SSH.
  • Authentication failures are visible as logon_failure events.
  • A firewall sits in front of the server and records blocked traffic.
  • You do not know the source IP, the accounts targeted, or whether it succeeded.
Investigation task

Your mission

  1. Locate the alertOn the SOC Triage Board, find the high-severity alert whose reasoning describes failed logons against a web server.
  2. Quantify the attackEstablish the source IP, the accounts attacked, the number of failures, and the exact start and end of the window.
  3. Confirm the outcomeDetermine whether any successful login followed from that source.
  4. Corroborate with the firewallCheck whether fw-01 recorded blocked connections from the same source to the same host.
  5. Test the false-positive theoryFind the account that also had failed logons that did not alert, and explain the difference.
Investigation workflow

Read, find, investigate, answer

This page is the brief. It tells you what happened and what you are being asked to work out. Nothing here is the answer — the SOC Dashboard and Kibana Discover are where you look it up.

  1. Read the scenarioUnderstand what you are being asked to investigate.

  4. Answer the questionsUse the evidence you found in Kibana to answer the investigation questions.

                    Progressive Hints

                    Need help?

                    Open one at a time — always try the SIEM first.
                    Hint 1 — finding the alert

                    Open the SOC Triage Board and use the "SIEM Alerts by Severity" panel. You want the alert with severity high and rule_name brute_force. The reasoning field already states the source IP, the host, the count and the window — read it first, then verify every number yourself in Discover.

                    Hint 2 — counting the failures

                    Do not read the count off the alert alone. Open the normalized-events-* data view in Discover and filter:

                    event_type: "logon_failure" AND host: "web-01" AND src_ip: "<ATTACKER_IP>"

                    Sort ascending by @timestamp. The first and last rows give you the window. Break the set down by user in the field list to get the per-account counts.

                    Hint 3 — the two thresholds

                    The brute_force rule has two configurable parameters, both in config/app-config.yaml: min_failures (how many) and time_window_minutes (over what period). Compare those two numbers against the activity you measured.

                    Hint 4 — firewall corroboration

                    The firewall events are event_type: "network_connection" on host fw-01 with severity: "high". They arrive from the same attacker IP within a couple of minutes of the SSH burst, which is what makes the correlation convincing: the attack is visible at two independent layers.

                    Hint 5 — the non-alerting account

                    Search for other logon_failure bursts on web-01 from inside the network. One account has repeated failures followed by a successful login. Count its failures and compare with min_failures. Also check when the failures occurred relative to the attacker: were they minutes or hours apart?

                    Hint 6 — shaping the finding

                    A strong finding names the activity, the target, the origin, the volume, the time window, the outcome, and the impact. It should also recommend the immediate next step for the SOC.
