Scenario 02 Medium Rule: successful_brute_force Expected severity: critical

Successful Login After Brute Force

The same failure pattern as Scenario 01 — except this time the attacker got in. Correlate the burst to the success and explain the escalation.

Incident Brief

Objective and background

Security objective

Prove that a failed-login burst ended in a successful authentication from the same source and for the same account, quantify the whole sequence, and state the resulting impact.

Incident story

An account owner reports that a colleague could not log in remotely and had to use the console instead. The SIEM has raised a critical alert. This is the single most important alert type in the lab: in Scenario 01 the attack failed, here it appears to have succeeded. Your job is to prove that, and to explain why the SIEM raised critical rather than high.

What you know at the start

  • One alert on the board is critical — the only one.
  • Authentication failures and successes both appear as events with the same src_ip.
  • You have not yet confirmed whether the successful login came from the attacker or from the legitimate user.

Investigation task

  1. Locate the critical alertFind it on the SOC Triage Board and read its reasoning.
  2. Separate it from Scenario 01The lab contains two brute force episodes. Make sure you are working the one that escalated.
  3. Build the sequenceList every failed logon in order, then the success, with timestamps.
  4. Measure the outcomeHow long between the first failure and the success? What are the rule's two time windows?
  5. Explain the escalationWhy does this alert read critical while the other reads high?
  6. State the impactThe attacker now has a valid session. What should the SOC do first?

                    Progressive Hints

                    Stuck? Reveal one step at a time.

                    Hint 1 — there are two brute force episodes

                    Open the "Top Source IPs by Alert Count" panel. Scenario 01's attacker and this scenario's attacker are different IP addresses. Use the SIEM Alerts by Severity panel and the time picker to isolate the critical alert, then confirm its rule_name.

                    Hint 2 — reading the escalation

                    Read the two reasoning sentences side by side. One says the threshold was exceeded; the other says the attack succeeded and states the number of failed logons that preceded it. The configuration parameter that governs the window is success_window_minutes.

                    Hint 3 — the evidence set

                    Do not assume the evidence count. Open the alert and count the values in evidence_event_ids, then load them:

                    _id: ("<FIRST_ID>", "<SECOND_ID>", ...)

                    Every one of them should belong to the same attack. Verify by checking that they share the same src_ip and host.

                    Hint 4 — the protocol detail

                    Look at the process object of the successful event, and at its raw_event. It names the service that handled the login and the authentication method. On the failed events there is no such process, because no session was ever created.

                    Hint 5 — containment

                    Think about what an attacker with a valid shell session can do next, and what the SOC must stop first: cut off the source, invalidate the credential, preserve the session and host state for forensics.
