Scenario 05 Hard All three rules Capstone

Multi-Event SOC Investigation

The whole incident. Reconstruct the complete timeline across every host, rank the findings by severity, and write the conclusion a SOC shift lead would escalate at handover.

Incident Brief

Objective and background

Security objective

Produce a single incident picture from four independent alerts across two hosts and two attacker source IPs, exclude the benign activity, and deliver a prioritised conclusion with justification.

Incident story

It is 08:00. You are the SOC analyst on shift. Overnight, the SOC Triage Board collected four alerts, and the operations team has separately reported that a web server account behaved strangely and that an application server administrator was seen at the keyboard. Before you escalate anything to the incident response team you must produce one coherent, evidence-based account of the night, and you must be explicit about what is noise.

The first four scenarios taught you each alert type individually. This scenario is the assessment: can you triage a whole queue, rank it, and write it up?

What you know at the start

  • Four alerts exist, across two distinct hosts and more than two distinct source IPs.
  • The same lab also contains benign activity that correctly produced no alert.
  • One alert is critical. The severity order is a triage order.

Investigation task

  1. Survey the queueCount the alerts and split them by severity and rule.
  2. Build the timelineOrder every relevant event on a single UTC axis, including the firewall records.
  3. Cluster the activityGroup events by source IP, host and user. Decide how many distinct activity clusters exist.
  4. Identify the baselineFind the routine activity on the web server and confirm it is unrelated.
  5. Identify the non-alerting near-missesTwo pieces of suspicious-looking activity produced no alert. Explain both.
  6. Rank and escalateDecide which finding is most serious and justify it with the alert severity and the evidence.
  7. Write the handoverProduce a short incident conclusion an oncoming analyst could act on.

                      Progressive Hints

                      Stuck? Reveal one step at a time.

                      Hint 1 — the master table

                      Go to the SOC Triage Board and export or read all four alerts. For each one note rule_name, severity, src_ip, user, host and timestamp. Do this before looking at any events — the alert queue is your starting position, not the evidence.

                      Hint 2 — the timeline

                      In Discover with the normalized-events-* data view, set the time picker to cover the whole incident and sort by @timestamp ascending. Read down the list. The two attacker IPs are the ones outside the internal 10.0.0.0/8 range that are involved in brute force.

                      Hint 3 — clustering

                      Group by src_ip first, then by host. Two alerts landed on web-01 but from different source IPs, for different users, and were generated by different rules. Ask yourself whether one attacker could plausibly have produced both, and whether the timeline supports or contradicts it.

                      Hint 4 — the non-alerting near-misses

                      Search for suspicious-looking activity that has no alert:

                      event_type: "logon_failure"

                      and

                      event_type: "process_create" AND process.name: "powershell.exe"

                      One of these sits below a numeric threshold; the other sits outside a time window. Both are the reason the rules do not fire on everything.

                      Hint 5 — ranking

                      Severity is a triage order, not a score. A critical alert means the attacker demonstrably succeeded; a high alert means something dangerous is in progress or just happened. Write down which of your alerts represents confirmed compromise rather than attempted or in-progress activity.

                      Hint 6 — the handover

                      A handover is not a summary of the dashboard. It answers: what is the impact, which assets are affected, what did the attacker achieve, what are they likely to do next, and what do we do first. Keep it to a short paragraph plus three actions.
